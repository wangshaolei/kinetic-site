LEG RAISE · AI REP COUNTER

Leg raise counter

KINETIC counts one leg raise when your hip angle drops to 100° or less and then comes back to 160° or more. Lying on the floor, side-on to the camera.

How KINETIC counts leg raises

The hip angle is the angle at your hip between torso and thigh (shoulder–hip–knee). With your legs straight and flat, your hip angle is about 180°; raised to vertical it is 90°. KINETIC counts at 100° and waits for your legs to lower past 160°.

KINETIC uses whichever side of your body the camera sees more clearly.

Where to put your phone

  1. Put the phone on the floor 1.5–2 m to your side, leaning against something so it stands up.
  2. Lie down with your body side-on to the camera.
  3. Check that your head, hips and feet are all in frame.

Good form

  • Lie flat and keep your legs as straight as you can.
  • Press your lower back into the floor while you lift.
  • Lower slowly; you don't need to touch the floor, just get past 160°.

Why a rep might not count

  • Your legs didn't rise to 100°.
  • You didn't lower your legs past 160° before the next rep.
  • Part of your body is out of frame or hidden. KINETIC pauses and shows a hint; those frames are skipped, and when you're back in view it carries on without counting the same rep twice.
  • The room is dark or there is a bright window behind you. Turn on a light and face away from the window.

Too strict or too loose?

Open the angle panel on the tracking screen and drag the thresholds. The defaults follow standard form with a little margin; beginners can loosen them, and you can tighten them as you get stronger. Each exercise keeps its own setting.
