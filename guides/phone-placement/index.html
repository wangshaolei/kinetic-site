GUIDE

Where to put your phone

Keep your whole body in frame with good light. Stand the phone upright 2–3 m away for standing exercises; for floor exercises like push-ups and sit-ups, put it on the floor beside you so it sees you from the side.

Rules for every exercise

  • Whole body in frame. KINETIC measures joint angles, so the joints it uses must stay visible for the whole rep. Most miscounts come from a hand, foot or head leaving the frame.
  • Good, even light. Turn on a light and don't stand with a bright window behind you.
  • One person in frame. If someone walks behind you, the app may track them instead.
  • A steady phone. Lean it against a wall, a water bottle or a stand. If the phone moves, the angles jump.
  • Fitted clothes help. Very loose clothing can hide where your knees and elbows are.

Standing, facing the camera

Used for: Squat, Jumping jack, Bicep curl, Lateral raise

  1. Stand the phone upright at about waist height.
  2. Step back 2–3 m so your whole body, head to feet, is in frame.
  3. Face the camera with your feet about shoulder-width apart.

Standing, facing the camera, weights at shoulder height

Used for: Overhead press

  1. Stand the phone upright at about waist height, 2–3 m away.
  2. Face the camera with your whole body in frame.
  3. Start with the dumbbells at shoulder height, wrists above elbows. If your arms hang down, the app waits.

Standing, side-on to the camera

Used for: Lunge

  1. Stand the phone upright at about waist height, 2–3 m away.
  2. Turn sideways so the camera sees your profile.
  3. Leave room in front and behind you so your stepping leg stays in frame.

Plank position, phone low at your side

Used for: Push-up

  1. Put the phone on the floor about 1.5 m to your side, leaning against something so it stands up.
  2. Turn your body side-on to the camera.
  3. Check that your head, hands and feet are all in frame.

Lying on the floor, side-on to the camera

Used for: Sit-up, Glute bridge, Leg raise

  1. Put the phone on the floor 1.5–2 m to your side, leaning against something so it stands up.
  2. Lie down with your body side-on to the camera.
  3. Check that your head, hips and feet are all in frame.

Hanging from a bar, facing the camera

Used for: Pull-up

  1. Place the phone in front of the bar, far enough back (usually 2–3 m) to see your hands on the bar.
  2. Face the camera.
  3. Check that your hands, elbows and body are all in frame while you hang.

Still not counting?

Watch the hint on the tracking screen: it tells you whether you're out of frame, facing the wrong way or not going deep enough. If your form is fine but reps are missed, open the angle panel and loosen the threshold a little.
